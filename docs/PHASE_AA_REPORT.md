# Phase AA report: real-world qualification, reproducibility, production evidence

This phase asked one question: **where is TraceJIT actually useful, and can someone
who did not write it reproduce the answer?** It did not ask for a faster TraceJIT,
and no product optimisation was implemented. The one product change on this branch
is a robustness fix that the harness found (A9 in [the ledger](PHASE_AA_LEDGER.md)).

Everything below comes from `benchmarks/results/phaseaa/`, generated by
`benchmarks/realworld_v2/` on the commits named in §1. Raw per-sample data is in
`results.json` and `raw/*.json`; the human-readable view is `results.md`.

## 3. Environment

`benchmarks/results/phaseaa/environment.json` records the full fingerprint.
Condensed: Ubuntu 24.04.5, kernel 6.1.102, glibc `2.39-0ubuntu8.12`, 2 vCPU, 3 GiB
RAM, ext4, shared VM (no dedicated core, no isolation from neighbours), gcc
13.3.0, Python 3.12, Node 26.13.1, hyperfine 1.19.0, `runtime allocator-entropy-v1
audited (8cebda42d140fc2b09639f8b13a5d5f2e617ef09)`, Landlock unavailable,
`proven` mode unavailable. clang, ninja, ccache, sccache, firebuild, perf, zstd,
clang-tidy, cppcheck, shellcheck, ruff and pyarrow are absent and are reported
`unavailable` — never `slower`.

The VM has two shared vCPUs and no frequency control. Repeatability was checked
rather than assumed: an identical warm hit measured 42 ms in a quiet session and
74–80 ms during the full run (§7). Absolute numbers on this host carry that
uncertainty; the paired design cancels most of it for the *ratios*.

## 4. Corpus

59 workload configurations in 12 families, frozen in `manifest.json`: 50 tier A
(strict native — no entropy substitution, no contract) and 9 tier C (declared
adaptations: a pinned environment, a pinned input, or a rewritten invocation).
Five of the 59 name tools that do not exist on this image and are reported as
`tool_unavailable`; they are excluded from eligibility denominators and stay
visible in every table.

Splits were declared before the run: dev 21, validation 22, held-out 16. The
held-out results were executed once, after the corpus was frozen, and are reported
separately. **No optimisation decision in this phase used any held-out number.**

Two orthogonal axes are reported as separate cells and never aggregated:

- *environment tier* — A (unmodified command, standard runtime) or C (declared
  adaptation, adaptation text in the manifest);
- *execution policy* — native, `allocator-entropy-v1` (the audited glibc
  allocator contract the beta shipped), `compiler-runtime-v1` (the contract that
  makes gcc's environment stable).

Tier D is what the tables call `refused`: a command whose behaviour cannot be
cached safely. Those rows are the negative result and are not dropped.

## 5. Eligibility

177 rows = 59 workloads × 3 policies, minus unavailable-tool rows.

| Tier / policy | Attempted | Reused | Refused | Other failure | Rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A, native | 49 | 1 | 47 | 1 | **2.0 %** |
| A, `allocator-entropy-v1` | 49 | 10 | 37 | 2 | **20.4 %** |
| A, `compiler-runtime-v1` | 49 | 17 | 30 | 2 | **34.7 %** |
| C, native | 5 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 0 % |
| C, `allocator-entropy-v1` | 5 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 0 % |
| C, `compiler-runtime-v1` | 5 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 0 % |

Reading this honestly:

- Under **unmodified native execution, TraceJIT is not usable for real workloads
  on this corpus**: 1 of 49. That one is a self-contained computation with no file
  inputs.
- The single beta-era "1/32" figure is not wrong, it is just the native cell; the
  contract cells are 10× and 17× larger on the same corpus. Reporting one
  "eligibility rate" without the policy attached would mislead in either
  direction.
- Tier C is 0/5 under every policy. Adapted interpreter workloads (CPython, NumPy,
  Pillow) refuse on clock reads, process identity or entropy even with the
  adaptation. Interpreters are not a viable target today.
- The dominant native blockers, from `aggregate.refusal_reasons`: randomness 23,
  unmodelled syscalls 16 (`getcwd`-class), `getrusage` 10, `clock_gettime` 8,
  `fstat` on a pipe 4, `uname`/system information 3, unrestorable output 3,
  process identity 2.

`compiler-runtime-v1` is a genuine widening (17/49) but it is a **different
contract with a different claim**, and the results below show it is not a free
win: it makes gcc re-usable and also makes cheap gcc invocations dramatically
slower when reused.

## 6. Measured performance

23 measured timing sessions covering 13 workloads (some workloads are timed under
two policies). Randomized paired samples, per-pair ratio bootstrap 95 % CI,
output deletion before every timed sample, HIT only when the decision was
`Reused`. >1 means the warm hit is faster than native.

| Workload | Policy | Native | Cold | Warm HIT | Ratio (95 % CI) | Break-even | 25× session |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `c-numeric-integration` | allocator | 344.9 ms | 384.2 | **11.4 ms** | **30.6×** (24.1–39.5) | 1 | 13.1× |
| `c-numeric-integration` | compiler | 355.9 ms | 379.4 | **14.5 ms** | **17.2×** (3.5–29.2) | 1 | 12.2× |
| `gcc-cc-heavy-tu` (zlib `deflate.c`) | compiler | 610.1 ms | 806.3 | **86.4 ms** | **7.35×** (5.3–9.3) | 1 | 5.30× |
| `zlib-compile-deflate` | compiler | 611.5 ms | 819.1 | **83.1 ms** | **6.92×** (5.3–8.8) | 1 | 5.43× |
| `zlib-compile-adler32` | compiler | 99.8 ms | 351.7 | 78.4 ms | 1.32× (1.17–1.44) | 12 | 1.12× |
| `gcc-noopt-compile` | compiler | 73.3 ms | 365.5 | 71.0 ms | 1.02× (0.87–1.17) | 128 | 0.89× |
| `gcc-cpp-preprocess` | compiler | 16.5 ms | 195.7 | 68.9 ms | 0.24× | — | 0.22× |

| `gcc-fanalyzer` | compiler | 26.7 ms | 202.8 | 72.2 ms | 0.37× | — | 0.34× |

| `sed-substitute-file` | allocator / compiler | 13.6–14.4 ms | 118–135 | 73.4–79.9 ms | 0.17–0.18× | — | 0.17–0.18× |
| `objdump-disassemble` | allocator / compiler | 4.5–4.7 ms | 54–59 | 17.4–18.8 ms | 0.25–0.26× | — | 0.23–0.24× |

**4 workloads have positive cumulative savings; 8 are slower than native; 14 rows
are losses.** The losses are not marginal: `readelf-header` and `cut-columns` are
6–7× slower warm, because a 2.5 ms command cannot pay a 16–22 ms hit.

`gcc-noopt-compile` is labelled *indistinguishable* (1.02×, CI 0.87–1.17, session
0.89×): the interval contains 1.0, so calling it either a win or a loss would be
reading noise. It is not counted as a win.

The floor is the story. On this host a warm hit costs **13–16 ms** for the
cheapest workloads, and 70–90 ms for anything gcc-shaped. Any command cheaper
than the floor loses, and most command-line tools are cheaper than the floor.

## 7. Cost model

Instrumented stage timing (`results.json` → `timing.cost_model.stages_ns`), read
as *relative* attribution only. It is **not additive with the timed wall clock**:
for `c-numeric` the instrumented stages sum to 21.6 ms against an 11.4 ms wall,
and for `sed` they sum to 24.5 ms against a 74–80 ms wall. `TRACEJIT_PHASE_TIMING=1`
perturbs the hit it measures (measured separately at +1.6 ms on a 42 ms hit), and
15–50 ms of the wall time is not attributed by any stage the harness can read.
That unattributed remainder is a finding, not a rounding error, and it is the
reason the cp3 target in §12 is what it is.

| Workload | startup | guard validation | decision persist | CAS restore | attributed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `c-numeric` (allocator) | 10.7 ms | 4.3 ms | 3.0 ms | 1.5 ms | 21.6 ms |
| `gcc-cc-heavy-tu` (compiler) | 10–11 ms | 52–72 ms | 8–23 ms | 3–18 ms | 72–90 ms |
| `zlib-compile-adler32` | 10–11 ms | 55–60 ms | 3–5 ms | 10–15 ms | ~80 ms |

Three things follow:

1. For compilers, **guard validation dominates**: hashing every recorded header
   and the toolchain binaries costs 52–72 ms, more than the compile it replaces in
   the cheap cases.
2. **Persisting a decision on every hit** costs 3–23 ms. That is pure overhead on
   the reuse path.
3. **Process startup** costs 8–11 ms, which alone exceeds the direct cost of most
   of the losing workloads.

Break-even: 1 repeat for the three large wins, 12 for `zlib-compile-adler32`, 128
for `gcc-noopt-compile`. Storage is 85 KB–2.8 MB per workload.

## 8. Correctness

The oracle (`oracle.py`) compares a full workspace snapshot — exit status, stdout,
stderr, relative paths of every created/modified/deleted file, their contents and
mode, plus covered metadata — between native and traced execution, with hard-link
groups compared as path sets. It does not consult TraceJIT's classification to
decide correctness; TraceJIT's own declarations are used only to separate
*covered* observations from *out-of-contract* ones.

- **0 incorrect HITs** across 177 rows (a `Reused` decision whose covered
  observations differ from native fails the run and would be listed in
  `aggregate.incorrect_hits`).
- Cache-state transitions were exercised per eligible workload: clean native,
  cold trace, first warm attempt, repeated hits, input mutation, expected
  deopt, immediate retry, output corruption, cache-object corruption, cache
  restart, input restored, candidate reuse.
- Output corruption splits by mechanism, and both outcomes are correct:
  `gcc`-created outputs (opened with `O_TRUNC`, never read) are **restored** from
  the cache; `gzip -f`, `bzip2 -f`, `objcopy` and `strip` read their existing
  output, so corrupting it changes a recorded dependency and correctly forces a
  **retrace**.
- Adversarial (`adversarial.json`): the preserved Phase W1 13-case matrix passes
  13/13, including the rename-source counterexample; 3 workload-specific mutation
  scenarios pass; **0 incorrect hits**; the TOCTOU race probe observed **0 stale
  results in 12 trials**, which is not evidence that the window is closed, only
  that this probe cannot find it.
- A same-size/same-timestamp rewrite could **not** be made to alias on this
  kernel/ext4: `os.utime` restores mtime but ctime moved measurably, so the guard
  saw the change. Recorded as an honest negative, not as proof of safety.

## 9. Findings about the product

1. **Missing output CAS object aborted the user's command** (`cache I/O failed…
   No such file or directory`, exit 1, command never executed) when the recorded
   output blob had been removed but the record survived. The stream path already
   retraced; the materialisation path did not. Fixed in `c3f77d8` (NotFound →
   `CorruptObject`, so the record is treated as corrupt and the command re-runs)
   with regression `a_missing_cached_output_object_retraces_instead_of_aborting`.
2. **Relocating `TRACEJIT_CACHE_DIR` invalidates every record**, because the
   inherited environment — including that variable — is hashed into the identity.
   A copied cache produces `Executed`, not a hit. Reproduced twice; not fixed;
   recorded as A8 in [the ledger](PHASE_AA_LEDGER.md) and as a blocker in
   [release readiness](RELEASE_READINESS_V03.md).
3. **`fs-identical-rewrite` is eligible and can never warm**: it rewrites its own
   input, so the recorded pre-state no longer holds once it has run.
4. **`nm-symbols` is `Guarded`, gets no hit**, and reproduces the documented
   metadata-guard limitation.
5. **Classification is not perfectly stable for `gcc-cc-small-tu` under
   `compiler-runtime-v1`**: it was `reused` in two full-suite runs and `refused`
   (`Nondeterministic`) in one, with an identical binary and identical corpus; a
   focused repeat gave 10/10 `Guarded`. The classification therefore depends on
   run conditions. Unresolved, and reported as a reproducibility risk rather than
   smoothed away.
6. **Tracing an ineligible command is expensive**: wrapping `make` costs
   **460 ms per invocation extra** (85.3 ms native vs 545.4 ms traced) on a full
   build. The wrapper should not be put in front of commands that are refused.

## 10. Where TraceJIT is actually useful

Ranked by reproducible net benefit on this evidence:

1. **Compute-bound commands whose output is much smaller than their cost, with
   no entropy- or clock-sensitive behaviour** — e.g. a numeric integration with no
   file inputs. 17–31× warm, break-even 1, and correctness is easy to establish.
2. **Large translation units under a declared compiler contract** — 6.9–7.4× warm,
   break-even 1, storage 2.8 MB. This is the credible niche: repeated compilation
   of an expensive TU, where the direct cost is ~0.6 s and the hit is ~85 ms.
   It requires the audited `compiler-runtime-v1` contract, so it is *not* strict
   native execution and must always be labelled as such.
3. **Repeated scientific/ETL compute with stable inputs** — plausible but not
   demonstrated here, because the interpreter workloads refused.

Where it does not belong, on the same evidence: short CLI tools (`cut`, `readelf`,
`objdump`, `sed`, `gzip` ≤ level 6), anything driven by `make`, and any
interpreter workload. There is no niche for TraceJIT as a transparent wrapper
around ordinary shell commands; the floor is larger than most commands.

## 11. Reproducibility

`benchmarks/realworld_v2/` is self-contained: `run.sh` fetches only the pinned
inputs it needs (SHA-256 for zlib 1.3.1 is
`9a93b2b7dfdac77ceba5a558a580e74667dd6fede4585b91eefb60f03b72df23`), builds the
corpus into a scratch directory, runs the three policies, and writes
schema-validated results plus a Markdown summary. `validate.py` enforces
`tracejit.realworld.results/v1`, including that rates equal reused/attempted and
that any incorrect hit fails. `AA_MODE=smoke` is the fast path used by CI.

A fresh-checkout run is recorded in [docs/BENCHMARK_REPRODUCTION.md](BENCHMARK_REPRODUCTION.md#phase-aa-fresh-checkout-check);
it is the procedure an outside reviewer should follow.

## 12. Verdict and next targets

**v0.3 is not qualified for release on this evidence** (see
[RELEASE_READINESS_V03.md](RELEASE_READINESS_V03.md)). The benchmark system is
qualified: it measures what it says, it fails closed on incorrect hits, and its
results are reproducible from a fresh checkout with one command. The product's
real-world applicability is narrow and contract-dependent, and the documentation
now says so.

Top three evidence-supported targets for Phase AB:

1. **Cut guard-validation cost for compiler workloads.** 52–72 ms of every gcc
   hit is re-hashing recorded inputs and binaries. A safe hierarchy (validated
   directory-level digests, deduplicated hashing, metadata fast path with a
   declared threat model) could halve the hit cost for the one workload class that
   already wins, turning 7× into something closer to 15× and making medium TUs
   (currently 1.0–1.3×) worth caching.
2. **Remove fixed hit overhead: decision persistence and startup.** 3–23 ms of
   persistence per hit and 8–11 ms of startup set the 13–16 ms floor that decides
   the entire "is this command too cheap" boundary. Both are pure overhead on the
   reuse path, and neither depends on the held-out results.
3. **Widen eligibility for real commands without weakening the contract**, starting
   with the refusal histogram: randomness 23, `getrusage` 10, `clock_gettime` 8,
   `getcwd`-class 16. A versioned `clocks-v1`/`usage-v1` contract with explicit
   substitution and a published failure model would move the 17/49 compiler cell
   and possibly some tier-C workloads — and the instability in §9.5 needs a
   deterministic classification test either way.

None of the three was implemented here. Each is stated with the measurement that
justifies it so Phase AB can be judged against the same protocol.
